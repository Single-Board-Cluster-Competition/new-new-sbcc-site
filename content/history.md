## SBCC 2023

On the 25th of May 2023, the university of California San Diego (UCSD) hosted
the first SBCC, open to all student teams who were interested in exploring and
learning.

Participants had the opportunity to show case their skills in building and
optimizing clusers composed of single board computers using the latest hardware
and software technologies. 

The event was held both virtually and in-person, with participants having the option to attend in person at UCSD or remotely via online platforms.
This ensured that the event was accessible to as many participants as possible, regardless of their location.

### Rules for 2023

As the first incarnation of the competition, SBCC23 had a limited set of hardware
restrictions: Apple M1/2 chips were off limits, 4 socket minimum per cluster,
$6000 USD MSRP budget limit, and a power limit of 250 watts,

The competition included benchmarking for
[HPL](https://netlib.org/benchmark/hpl/),
[HPGC](https://www.hpcg-benchmark.org/), 
[Intel MPI benchmarks](https://github.com/intel/mpi-benchmarks) and
[Graph500](https://graph500.org/). A reference implementation for Graph500 weere
in the form of [ParallelFirstSearch](https://github.com/pohaoc/ParallelFirstSearch).
And in the spirit of running on single board computers, efficiency of each cluster
normalized their HPL results with [sample results](https://github.com/deater/performance_results),
providing a table for chips and their GFLOPS/watt.

### Winners of SBCC2023

The Supercomputer Club at Aalborg University emerged as the winners of SBCC23.
In the HPL benchmark, they delivered a 20-fold increase in GFLOPS compared to the second-place finisher.
